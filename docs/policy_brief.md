# Policy Brief: Strengthening SB 375 via SB 1087 Guardrails

## 1. Executive Summary
SB 375 created planning mandates (Sustainable Communities Strategies) to align transportation with climate goals. However, a significant "implementation gap" persists: while regions project VMT reductions in plans, project-level investments continue to prioritize highway capacity expansion. SB 1087 provides a crucial opportunity to establish performance-based funding guardrails and close this gap.

## 2. The Implementation Gap
Despite aggressive adopted targets, per-capita VMT continues to rise or stagnate in many regions. 
- Disconnect between theoretical model outputs (SCS) and actual funding pipelines (RTIP/ITIP).
- Negative consequences disproportionately fall on vulnerable communities (air quality, safety) while failing to deliver long-term congestion relief (induced demand).

## 3. The Three Pillars of Reform (SB 1087)
SB 1087 leverages Government Code 65080 and Streets and Highways Code 2032 to enforce outcomes:

### Pillar 1: "Corrective Action" Moratorium
Regions missing VMT targets for two consecutive cycles trigger a "corrective action" status, halting capacity-increasing highway funding until trajectory is corrected.

### Pillar 2: VMT Neutrality & Mitigation Banking
Any capacity-adding projects must demonstrate VMT neutrality over a 20-year horizon. Unavoidable increases must be offset via a mitigation bank (e.g., AB 130 Statewide VMT Mitigation Bank) using standard NCST Induced Travel penalty math.

### Pillar 3: Objective Need & Climate Floor
A statewide project scoring system prioritizing VMT reduction, safety, and air quality. Projects generating a net-negative effect (VMT increases, emissions spikes) fall below the "climate floor" and become ineligible for state funding.

## 4. Peer-State Precedents
These guardrails are legally sound and modeled after successful implementations in peer states:
- **Colorado:** GHG standard with budget lock mechanism for non-compliant MTIPs.
- **Minnesota:** GHG Impact Assessment with a capacity-expansion trigger enforcing offsets.
- **Virginia:** SMART SCALE explicit scoring mechanisms prioritizing safety, cost-benefit, and environmental metrics.
- **Massachusetts:** Enforceable DOT emission caps directly tied to project programming.
